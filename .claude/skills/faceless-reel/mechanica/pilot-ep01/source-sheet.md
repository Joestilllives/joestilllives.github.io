# Source sheet: How a key actually opens a lock

Mechanica, episode 1. Every factual statement in the video, in the order it is spoken, with the source and the passage it rests on. The patents are 19th-century scans; quotes are transcribed from the scans, with "..." where we skip text.

**"Inside the lock is a plug that has to turn, and stacks of pins pushed down by springs."**

- Claim: Pins sit in drilled holes with drivers above them and springs that push them.
- Source: Linus Yale Jr., US Patent 31,278, published 1861-01-29, [https://patents.google.com/patent/US31278A/en](https://patents.google.com/patent/US31278A/en)
- Passage: "...the pistons..., their drivers..., and their actuating springs..., which are placed in cylindrical holes..., drilled for that purpose in the pin-chamber..."

**"Each stack is split in two, and every stack splits at a different height."**

- Claim: Each pin stack has two parts with a line of division between them.
- Source: Linus Yale Jr., US Patent 48,475 'Improvement in locks', published 1865-06-27, [https://patents.google.com/patent/US48475A/en](https://patents.google.com/patent/US48475A/en)
- Passage: "...the various lines of division between the two parts of the tumblers..."

**"The right key lifts every split to one exact line. Now the plug turns."**

- Claim: The stacks are different lengths, and the key must line up every division before the cylinder can turn.
- Source: Linus Yale Jr., US Patent 48,475, published 1865-06-27, [https://patents.google.com/patent/US48475A/en](https://patents.google.com/patent/US48475A/en)
- Passage: "[The tumblers] are of different lengths, and must all be arranged by the key, so that the various lines of division between the two parts of the tumblers are all in the same line before the cylinder D can be turned."

**"Linus Yale Junior described exactly this in his 1865 patent."**

- Claim: Linus Yale Jr. described this in a patent published in 1865.
- Source: Linus Yale Jr., US Patent 48,475, published 1865-06-27, [https://patents.google.com/patent/US48475A/en](https://patents.google.com/patent/US48475A/en)
- Passage: Patent record: L. Yale Jr., publication date 1865-06-27.

## What we chose not to claim

- That most locks today use this design (only secondary sources found)
- How many pins a typical lock has (only secondary sources found)
- Who invented the pin tumbler first (history is disputed; Stansbury 1805 and Yale Sr. 1848 precede this patent)

## Simplifications in the animation

- The video says 'each stack is split in two'. In the patent the 'two parts of the tumblers' are what are now called the key pin and driver pin. Accurate.
- The video shows one plug rotating 90 degrees. Real locks connect the plug to a cam that moves the bolt; we do not show or claim that step.
