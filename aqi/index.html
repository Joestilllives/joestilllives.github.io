    Each number is the Indian AQI sub-index for that pollutant. The overall AQI is the highest sub-index.

    How this works

    1. One scale. Many apps show the US AQI, which runs much higher than India's for the same air. Wherever we have raw concentrations, we recompute the Indian National AQI (CPCB breakpoints) ourselves.
    2. Side by side, never blended. Every source keeps its own number, timestamp and distance so you can judge it.
    3. Agreement score. High when sources are within ~20% of each other, Medium within ~40%, Low beyond that.

    Caveats

    • CPCB sub-indices are based on 24-hour averages (8-hour for O₃ and CO), as published.
    • Open-Meteo / CAMS is a satellite-assisted model at ~40 km resolution. It's good for regional trends but can miss local hotspots. We average its last 24 hours to match CPCB's method.
    • WAQI mostly republishes government stations, so it's a cross-check of the data pipeline, not a fully independent measurement. Its values are back-converted from the US scale and are approximate.
    • Citizen sensors (OpenAQ) are latest readings, not 24-hour averages, and low-cost sensors tend to read high in humid air.

    Data: CPCB via data.gov.in · Copernicus CAMS via Open-Meteo · World Air Quality Index Project · OpenAQ. Not a medical or official advisory.
